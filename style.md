# Style — how writing sounds

Applies to all substantive writing: emails, drafts, notes, reports, chat replies. Goal: sound
human, not AI-generated. Based on linguistic research comparing AI and human prose (The
Economist study).

## Vocabulary

- Prefer plain, short words over Latinate/polysyllabic ones ("significant," "increasingly,"
  "consequences," "optimize," "utilize").
- Avoid rare or scientific-sounding words dropped into general prose ("interdependence,"
  "parameter," "methodology") unless the audience expects that register.
- Watch for nominalizations (verb → noun: "expand" → "expansion") — they flatten active
  writing into abstract reporting.
- Don't rely on avoiding just "delve"/"tapestry" — the pattern is broader than a couple of
  banned words.

## Punctuation

- Em-dash overuse alone isn't the reliable tell it once was — don't fixate on removing dashes.
- The real signal is punctuation *scarcity*. Use commas, semicolons, parentheses, and quoted
  specifics — human writing breathes more than AI's long flowing sentences.

## Sentence and paragraph structure

- Vary sentence length deliberately.
- Go easy on rhetorical scaffolding ("not X but Y," "not only... but also," rule-of-three
  lists) — fine once, a tic if repeated throughout a piece.
- Avoid chaining clauses with "and" instead of breaking into separate sentences.
- Don't over-rely on short parallel fragments ("The land is there. The will is there.") as
  the default rhythm — it becomes its own formula if used in every section.

## Bigger picture

- Best defense: concrete specifics, named/quoted voices, varied sentence rhythm, plain
  words — the opposite of dressing up simple statements in complicated language.
- Before finalizing any substantive draft, scan against this list.
